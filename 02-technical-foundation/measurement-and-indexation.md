# Measurement & Indexation Foundation

## Why we started here

The first instinct in SEO is often to start with keywords and content. That would have been the wrong first move. At the beginning of the project:

- Google Search Console was not properly implemented
- GA4 and Google Tag Manager were not properly set up
- Approximately 70% of the website was not indexed
- Organic performance could not be measured reliably

Before scaling content, we first needed to understand what Google could see, what users were doing and where the biggest technical constraints existed. Our first phase therefore focused on:

**Measurement → Visibility → Indexation → Prioritisation**

---

## 1. Building the measurement foundation

We implemented and structured the core measurement stack around:

- Google Search Console
- Google Analytics 4
- Google Tag Manager

Each tool had a different role.

### Google Search Console

Search Console became the primary source for understanding organic search visibility. We used it to monitor:

- Indexed versus non-indexed pages
- Search impressions
- Organic clicks
- Search queries
- Landing-page visibility
- Indexation issues
- Changes in organic performance

This gave us visibility into what was happening before a user reached the website.

---

### Google Analytics 4

GA4 gave us the next layer. Instead of stopping at rankings or clicks, we could look at what happened after users arrived. This allowed us to connect organic acquisition with:

- Landing-page traffic
- User behaviour
- Engagement
- Customer journeys
- Conversion activity

The objective was to move the SEO discussion from: **Are rankings improving?** toward: **Is organic search bringing more relevant users into the business?**

---

### Google Tag Manager

Google Tag Manager gave us more control over tracking implementation. It created a stronger foundation for measuring important behaviour and improving the setup without treating every tracking change as a separate website development project. Together, the measurement stack became:

+ **Search Console → Search visibility**
+ **GA4 → Website behaviour**
+ **GTM → Tracking infrastructure**

---

## 2. Making indexation visible

Once Search Console was properly available, one of the largest issues became clear. Approximately:

**70% of the website was not indexed**

This fundamentally changed our priorities. Publishing additional content while most of the existing website was not properly visible to Google would have increased output without necessarily increasing organic growth. We therefore treated indexation as a growth constraint.

---

## 3. Prioritising the pages that mattered

Not every URL has the same commercial value. The objective was therefore not: **Get every possible URL indexed.** The objective was: **Make sure the pages that matter for search and the customer journey can be discovered, understood and indexed.** We started looking at the website through different page types.

Examples included:
- Vacancy pages
- Service pages
- Commercial landing pages
- Informational content
- Company pages

This helped us distinguish between pages that supported organic acquisition and pages that existed mainly for informational or operational reasons.

---

## 4. From website pages to search assets

A major shift was changing how we looked at individual pages. Previously, many pages were simply pieces of website content. Under the new model, important pages needed to have a clear role within the organic growth system. For every important page, we increasingly asked:

1. Who is this page for?
2. What problem or search intent does it address?
3. Should Google index it?
4. How will users discover it?
5. Where does it sit within the website structure?
6. What should the user do next?

This changed the mindset from: **Website maintenance** to: **Organic acquisition architecture**

---

## 5. Indexation became a management KPI

Indexation was no longer treated as an invisible technical issue. It became part of the SEO operating model. We could monitor:

**Important pages created** → **Pages discoverable** → **Pages indexed** → **Pages receiving impressions** → **Pages generating clicks** → **Pages contributing traffic** → **Pages supporting conversion**

This made it easier to identify where growth was being blocked.

For example:

- A page with no indexation has a technical problem before it has a ranking problem.
- An indexed page with no impressions may have a relevance or demand problem.
- A page with impressions but few clicks may have a SERP or intent problem.
- A page with traffic but little commercial value may have a conversion or journey problem.

This became a much more useful way to diagnose SEO performance.

---

## 6. The order of operations

Our sequence became:

### Step 1 | Measure
Build reliable visibility through Search Console, GA4 and GTM.

### Step 2 | Diagnose
Understand which pages were indexed, which were not and where organic growth was being constrained.

### Step 3 | Prioritise
Focus first on pages and sections with the highest search and commercial potential.

### Step 4 | Improve
Address the technical, structural and content issues limiting visibility.

### Step 5 | Measure again
Use the new data to determine the next highest-impact opportunity.

The process became iterative: **Measure → Diagnose → Fix → Validate → Learn → Prioritise**

---

## Why this mattered

The most important change was not the installation of three analytics tools. It was that SEO became measurable. Instead of discussing organic growth through assumptions, we could start making decisions using actual search and website data. And instead of immediately investing in more content, we first worked on making sure the existing website had the foundation required to grow. That foundation became the starting point for the increase from approximately:

**2,000 → 6,500 monthly organic visitors within six months.**
