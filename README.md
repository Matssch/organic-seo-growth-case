# Organic SEO Growth Case
**How my team and I grew organic traffic from approximately 2,000 to 6,500 monthly visitors in six months by rebuilding the SEO foundation of a Dutch staffing business.**

## Headline results
| KPI | Starting point | Six months later |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monthly organic traffic | ~2,000 | **~6,500** |
| Organic traffic growth | Baseline | **+225%** |
| Website performance score | ~40 | **~88** |
| Website indexed | ~30% | Technical/indexation issues actively addressed |
| Search Console | Not properly implemented | **Implemented** |
| GA4 / GTM | Not properly implemented | **Implemented** |
| Website model | Online flyer | **Search-led acquisition platform** |

The result was not driven by one SEO trick. It came from improving the system behind organic growth:

**Measurement → Technical foundation → Indexation → Site architecture → Search intent → Content → Traffic → Conversion**

---

## The challenge
The website already generated organic traffic, but SEO was not yet operating as a structured growth channel. Several fundamental issues limited its potential:

- Approximately **70% of the website was not indexed**
- Google Search Console was not properly implemented
- GA4 and GTM were not properly implemented
- Website performance was approximately **40**
- Duplicate pages and redirect issues existed
- Titles, meta descriptions and heading structures needed improvement
- Internal linking was weak
- URLs were not always structured clearly
- Vacancies had limited visibility within the website
- Content primarily explained what the company did instead of solving the user's search need

The website largely functioned as an online company flyer. The opportunity was to turn it into an organic acquisition platform.

---

## The strategic shift
The biggest change was how we thought about the website.

### Before
**Company → Services → Information**

### After
**Search need → Relevant page → Solution → Next step**

Instead of starting with: What do we want to tell people?

we increasingly started with: What is the user actually looking for?

That shift influenced technical SEO, content, website architecture, vacancies, internal linking and conversion journeys.

---

## What we changed
### 1. Built the measurement foundation
We implemented and structured:

- Google Search Console
- Google Analytics 4
- Google Tag Manager

This gave us visibility into search performance, organic landing pages, user behaviour and technical issues.

---

### 2. Addressed the technical SEO foundation
Together with web development, we worked on:

- Indexation issues
- Redirects and broken URLs
- Duplicate pages
- URL structures
- Titles and meta descriptions
- Heading structures
- Internal linking
- Website performance

Performance improved from approximately: **40 → 88**, through improvements including image optimisation, code optimisation and caching.

---

### 3. Rebuilt the site around search demand
The website needed to become easier to understand for both users and search engines. We increased the focus on:

- Search intent
- Vacancy visibility
- Clear page roles
- Logical website structure
- Internal linking
- Relevant landing pages
- Clearer user journeys

The website moved from a company-led structure toward a search-led structure.

---

### 4. Changed the content model
The original content often started with: **"This is what we do."**
We shifted toward: **"This is the problem you are trying to solve."**

Content became increasingly driven by:

- Search need
- User intent
- Relevance
- Solution
- Next action

This connected SEO more closely to acquisition and conversion.

---

### 5. Turned SEO into a team operating model
SEO was not treated as a separate specialist project. Marketing, content and web development worked together around shared priorities. Our loop became:

**Measure → Identify opportunity → Prioritise → Improve → Validate → Learn**

Marketing owned the growth direction and prioritisation. Web development supported the technical implementation. The marketing team worked across content, on-page SEO, WordPress, measurement and optimisation.

---

## My role

I led the SEO direction and prioritisation together with my marketing team. My role included:

- Identifying organic growth opportunities
- Setting SEO priorities
- Connecting search demand to website and content strategy
- Leading improvements to the measurement foundation
- Prioritising technical improvements with web development
- Working with the team on content and on-page optimisation
- Monitoring organic performance
- Connecting SEO to wider marketing and business objectives

The result was a **team effort**. My responsibility was to make sure the team focused on the highest-impact growth opportunities rather than treating SEO as a disconnected list of tasks.

---

## The result
Within six months:

### Organic traffic
**~2,000 → ~6,500 monthly visitors**

**+225% growth**

### Website performance
**~40 → ~88**

At the same time, the company gained:

- A stronger technical SEO foundation
- Proper organic measurement
- Better indexation visibility
- A clearer website architecture
- More search-led content
- Stronger internal linking
- A repeatable SEO operating process

The result was not simply more traffic. It was a stronger organic growth engine.

---

## How I measure SEO

I do not see rankings as the final KPI. The measurement model is:

**Indexation** → **Impressions** → **Clicks** → **Organic traffic** → **Landing-page behaviour** → **Conversion** → **Commercial value**

The further SEO can be connected to commercial outcomes, the more useful the channel becomes.

## What I would build next
The first six months proved that the organic channel could grow. The next stage would focus on connecting that growth more deeply to business value through:

- Organic conversion measurement
- Keyword and topic clusters
- Search Console opportunity analysis
- Content refresh programmes
- Structured SEO experimentation
- Stronger technical monitoring
- Organic lead and application quality
- Organic CAC and commercial contribution

The maturity model becomes:

**Technical SEO → Organic traffic → Conversion → Quality → Commercial value**

---

## Core principle
**SEO is not a collection of optimisations. It is a growth system.**

+ Technical SEO creates the foundation.
+ Search intent creates relevance.
+ Content captures demand.
+ Site architecture connects it.
+ Measurement tells us where to invest next.
